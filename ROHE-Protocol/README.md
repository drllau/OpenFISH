# Record/Observe - Hysteresis Exclusion

## Concept

The Hanoi Cybercrime Convention (HCC) alacks the safeguards of Council of Europe Convention on Cybercrime ([CECoC](## "also known as Budapest Convention")). This computational law designs/drafts a G4T2P (govt for thing) protocol which addresses the discrepencies between NZ [Budapest enactment](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/bill/government/2024/0081/39.0/whole.html) and Australia's entry to the HCC. We take as starting point the AustLII [Cybercrime map](https://austlii.community/wiki/CyberLaw/AustralianCyberLawMap) with plausible AU extrapolations for preservation, production, and (contra-)protection _judicial orders_ over surveillance devices. 

## What is a surveillance device?

In the broadest sense it is any "Thing" (presumptive not a "Person") in the digi-physical world which can resolve questions of fact for judicial proceedings. The common interogatives are:

- **when** - "on or around [Date/Time]" such as metadata for cellphone tower connectivity;
- **where** - at or about [Location/Landmark] such as geo-encoded imagery from bodycams;
- **who** - personally identifiable information such as face/fingerprint scanners at checkpoints.

It is a question of law as to the bright line between active surveillance and passive monitoring. The ROHE protocol applies 3 basic dimensions, the threshold of which is left to competent institutions to qualify:
- reach/proximity
- time-shift
- hysteresis excision

<detail><summary>Example might be a film crew </summary>
... but the recording should have waivers for recognisable bystanders/scenes (cf trademark laws), or are incidental to the broadcast, and would not be a surveillance device no matter how intrusive it may appear.

A drone which overflies a "protected" locale "accidently" leaving camera on is a surveillance device because time-shifting the footage changes the intent from mere navigation or proximate collision avoidance.

A valid protection order such as for domestic violence avoidance may enable a normal device such as smart-phone to become a surveillance device as it preserves evidence of breach so long as not proven to be tampered with (eg deep-fake).

## Entrenched Prohibition against General Warrants

The [Te Tiriti o Waitangi](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1975/0114/latest/whole.html "Waitangi Treaty") and NZ Bill of Rights ([BoRA](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1990/0109/latest/DLM224792.html)) preserves the fundamental freedom of Persons to 
1. Innate - Life, Limb & Liberty
  1. s8 (life) & s11 (refusal of medical treatment)
  2. s9 (torture), s10 (medical experiment)
  3. s16 (assembly), s17 (association), s18 (movement)
2. Inherent - Conscience, Civics, Culture
  1. s13 (thought), s15 (religion)
  2. s12 (vote), s19 (non-discrimination)
  3. s14 (expression) s20 (culture)
3. Imposed - Immunity, Injury, Inurity 
  1. s21 (unreasonable search), s22 (arbitrary detention) s24 (non-causal charge)
  2. s21 (unreasonale seizure) s23 (uncounselled defense) s27 (unreviewable recourse)
  3. s25 (fairness) s26 (non retroactive) s24 (non-capricious)

It is noteworthy that International Convention on Civil & Political Rights (ICCPR art 17) has _No one shall be
subjected to arbitrary or unlawful interference with his privacy, ... _ is precluded from the BORA. However if lawful state surveillance (eg sex offender lists) are an injury to a Person's innate rights (eg liberty of movement in approaching protected locales), then such inurities must
- be reasonable (s21) and not arbitrary (duality of detention)
- be subject to review (s27) if challenged on inherent grounds (eg pervasive surveillance creating such fear as to prevent voting)
- be subject to standards of fairness (s25) and not capricious or unconnected with a lawful cause of action.

Note that BORA would apply to surveillance from:
1. devices owned, operated or occupied (justifiable interception) by a qualifying state actor in [public space](https://www.gcsb.govt.nz/assets/MPS/MPS-Reissue-2025-Conducting-Surveillance-in-a-Public-Place.pdf)
2. electronic information acquired, arranged, or accepted (tipoffs) from non-state actors, private or otherwise;
3. 4th party sources, software or seizures that are subject to judicial preservation or production orders.

This information would normally be excluded as a general warrant [Entick v. Carrington](## "[1765] 95 ER 807, King’s Bench") or tresspass via thematic warrants [R v IPT](https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2018-0004)
